The card fee alone is roughly 40 times the spot taker fee on the same amount. That gap is why "which exchange has the cheapest USDT" is the wrong question and "which funding rail can I use" is the right one.

## Getting the USDT out again

Buying and holding on an exchange is the easy half. Moving funds is where mistakes become permanent.

From the web: **Assets → Withdraw**, select USDT, choose on-chain withdrawal, then enter the address, the network, and the amount. Check every field before confirming. Arrival takes minutes and depends on the network you chose. The same flow exists in the wallet, with the same warning attached: the address and the network must match.

A few things worth knowing before your first transfer:

- **Network fees go to the blockchain, not to OKX.** OKX charges no platform fee on some USDT transfers, but the network fee still applies, and it is shown on the confirmation screen.
- **Wallet-to-wallet and wallet-to-exchange transfers both pay that fee.**
- **Cross-chain moves are not swaps.** USDT on Ethereum cannot be sent to a Bitcoin address or converted into BTC by the network itself. The chains USDT is issued on include Ethereum, Tron, Solana, Algorand and BSC, and each one needs a matching address.
- **A wrong-network transfer is not reversible.** There is no support ticket that fixes it.

One distinction that most buying guides skip: **arriving in your account and being withdrawable are two different states.** Binance.US, for example, holds funds deposited by ACH, debit card or Apple Pay, plus any crypto bought with them, for seven days before withdrawal. OKX's hold policy depends on your region and payment rail, so check the terms attached to the method you use before you count on same-day withdrawal.

## FAQ

**How much do I need to start?**
OKX's walkthrough does not publish a minimum. In practice the floor is set by the payment method: card purchases have issuer minimums, P2P ads have merchant minimums, and bank rails have their own. For reference, Coinbase states $1 and Binance.US states $2.

**How long until the USDT shows up?**
Card purchases and P2P releases are typically immediate to a few minutes. Bank transfers depend on the rail. On-chain delivery after a cash purchase can take up to 20 minutes in OKX's Southeast Asia setup.

**Can I withdraw it right away?**
Not always. Some funding methods carry a hold, and Binance.US's seven-day rule is the clearest published example. Check the terms for your method before assuming instant withdrawal.

**Why did I receive slightly less than I paid?**
Either a fee was deducted, or the quoted rate included a spread. On a $1,000 card purchase at 3.99%, about $39.90 goes to the card rail before any crypto is bought.

**Can USDT drop below $1?**
It has traded away from $1 before, and OKX's FAQ says so directly. The all-time high of $1.22 is the other direction of the same fact.

**Should I move it to my own wallet?**
Leave it on the exchange if you are trading within days. Move it to a self-custody or hardware wallet if you plan to hold a large balance, and budget the network fee for the transfer.
